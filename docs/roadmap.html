02

The five decisions, and what was decided

Each of these shapes the database and the API. They were settled before phase 1 precisely so they would not have to be unpicked afterwards.

What token does the merchant receive?

This is the central constraint of the non-custodial model, and it deserves to be stated plainly: because we never hold the money, the merchant receives exactly what the customer sent. If the customer sends BNB and the merchant wants USDT, no conversion happens — converting means holding, and holding means custody.

Cryptomus solves this with an internal ledger ("instant conversion to stablecoin"). We cannot, short of becoming custodial or putting a DEX swap in the forwarder path, which brings slippage, MEV and contract complexity.

decided No swap in v1. A merchant enables only the tokens they are willing to hold. In practice most merchants will accept USDT and USDC only, which is entirely acceptable. Swap is deferred past beta.

Invoices priced in fiat or in token?

Both are needed and their risk behaviour differs. amount_fiat means "$20", converted to a token amount when the invoice is created and locked for a TTL — the volatility risk inside that window sits with the merchant. amount_token means "exactly 20 USDT", with no FX risk at all.

decided Both, from phase 2, via a pricing_mode column. Shipped, along with a third mode, fixed_rate, for a merchant's own token.

Who pays the network fee?

As the Cryptomus analysis showed, no serious gateway absorbs a variable blockchain fee. Absorbing it means unbounded gas risk on your own balance sheet.

decided The settlement cost is deducted from the merchant's amount and shown openly in the dashboard, with the acceptance percentage listed separately. On TON, where settlement is free, it is zero — and that is itself a sales argument, which is why the checkout page shows the figure per network.

Are Stars and crypto one product or two surfaces?

Telegram's Bot Platform rules restrict Mini Apps to TON assets, and require digital goods sold inside Telegram to use Stars exclusively. A Mini App advertising USDT on Ethereum risks a ban — independent of where the company is registered.

decided One merchant identity and shared reporting, but two separate checkout paths with a feature flag on what each surface displays. Detail in phase 10.

Bitcoin: custodial, xpub co-signing, or drop it?

Bitcoin has no equivalent of the Forwarder's immutable destination. Aggregating UTXOs requires the spending key for the deposit addresses, which makes that path custodial unless the addresses are derived from the merchant's own xpub and the merchant co-signs — which is a heavy experience.

decided Dropped from v1. This is enforced by the type system rather than by convention: ChainId has no bitcoin member, so nothing can reference it by accident.

The surfaces the product is made of

Checkout page

What the end customer sees. Network choice ranked by FeePolicy, QR, address and memo, the rate-lock countdown, live status, and the underpaid path. Built; mobile refinement still to come.

Merchant dashboard

Signup, payout address, network and token selection, adding a custom contract, payment list and detail, payout history, webhook log, API keys, reports.

API and SDKs

REST with idempotency keys, signed webhooks, SDKs for Node, PHP and Python, and documentation. This is what makes AVEX a gateway rather than a payment form.

Telegram surface

A bot issuing XTR invoices for Stars, and a Mini App restricted to TON and jettons. Its lifecycle and its rules are entirely separate from the crypto path.

Admin panel

For the AVEX team, not the merchant: contract review queue, settlement monitoring, unmatched-payment reconciliation, system health, merchant suspension.

Price engine

Multiple sources, median, staleness detection, circuit breaker, and a fixed-rate mode for a merchant's own token. Every financial decision depends on it.

04

The price engine

Separated out because it is the one place where a simple bug turns directly into lost money. If the rate is wrong, a merchant hands over goods for less than they should have, and nobody notices until the end of the month.

CoinGecko Binance Kraken → median + outlier reject → staleness gate → quote lock (TTL)

Several sources, take the median

Never rely on one source. Take the median, and if dispersion exceeds a threshold, close that asset temporarily. An API returning a wrong number is worse than an API that is down.

Staleness matters more than precision

Every rate carries a timestamp, and past a threshold no new invoice is issued. Caching is necessary because free CoinGecko is rate-limited, but a cache without timestamps is exactly what leads to mispricing.

Rate lock and spread

An invoice locks the rate for its TTL. Inside that window the volatility risk is the merchant's, so a merchant can set a protective spread (say 50bps). Without it, a volatile market bleeds them slowly.

Fixed rate for a merchant's own token

A merchant's own token has no price on any exchange. fixed_rate lets the merchant set the rate themselves — with a validity date and a change log, because that number is money.

The circuit breaker is required, not optional

If the sources disagree badly or all go down, the system must stop issuing new invoices rather than carry on with the last known rate. Invoices already in flight complete against their own locked rate. That is the difference between a few minutes of downtime and a loss.

05

Accepting a custom contract

Merchants want to add their own token. The capability is necessary, and it is exactly where an attacker walks in: anyone can deploy a contract called USDT. The system must never grant standing to a contract that has not been reviewed.

Two rules hold the whole thing together. An unknown result never reads as absent — a probe that could not determine whether a token takes a fee on transfer is not a token that doesn't. And clean checks never accumulate into approval: the curated list is the only path to approved status.

CheckWhat it catchesIf it fails
Code present at the addressA wrong address, or an ordinary walletAutomatic rejection
ERC-20 probeDo symbol, decimals and transfer genuinely exist?Automatic rejection
Decimals sanityAbsurd values that break the arithmeticManual review
Fee on transferA token where less arrives than was sentHigh-risk flag · higher tolerance required
RebasingA balance that changes on its ownHigh-risk flag
Proxy or upgradeableA contract that can change behaviour tomorrowManual review
pause or blacklistThe issuer's ability to freeze transfersDisclosed to the merchant
Liquidity depthA token with no market, whose price is meaninglessForced into fixed_rate

Detection is done by scanning the bytecode for issuer-control selectors, reading the EIP-1967 proxy slots, and simulating a transfer through eth_call with a state override, so a fee-on-transfer token is caught by observing the arithmetic rather than by trusting the source.

Two entries changed during the build, both for reasons worth recording rather than quietly overwriting.

07

Twelve phases

Ordered by dependency, not importance. Phase 4 was the real milestone: from that point the product moves real money, albeit on one network only. Phases 6 onward are detailed feature by feature in the panels document.

01done

Platform foundation

complete

The infrastructure everything else sits on. No payment logic here.

  • Monorepo, CI, migrations
  • Auth: email and password, email verification, TOTP second factor
  • Organisation, membership and role model (owner, admin, developer, viewer)
  • Scoped API keys, test and live modes, rotatable
  • Immutable audit log for every settings change
  • Rate limiting and per-merchant data isolation

exit criterion — met A user signs up, enables a second factor, creates an API key, and everything they did appears in the audit log. Two defects were caught here by the integration suite: elevation was being granted to accounts with no authenticator, and the second-factor check ran before the role check.

02done

Price engine

complete

Before anything else, because invoice pricing depends on it.

  • Multiple price sources with median and outlier rejection
  • Staleness gate and circuit breaker
  • Rate lock on the invoice with a TTL and configurable spread
  • fiat, token and fixed_rate modes
  • Integer-only money: RATE_SCALE 1e18, FIAT_SCALE 1e6. Amounts owed round up, valuations round down.

exit criterion — met With two of three sources cut off by hand, the system stops issuing invoices and those in flight complete intact.

03done

Asset registry and contract vetting

complete

The global asset list, and the path for a merchant to add their own contract.

  • Published global list (USDT, USDC and the native asset of each network)
  • Automatic ERC-20 probe, fee-on-transfer, rebase and proxy detection
  • Risk score and review queue
  • Merchant-scoped visibility for an unapproved token
  • An illiquid token is forced into fixed_rate

exit criterion — met A deliberately fee-on-transfer contract is flagged and is not auto-approved. Two schema defects were caught before shipping: booleans stored as text, and a unique index that allowed several native assets per network because Postgres treats NULLs as distinct.

04done

Vertical slice: one network, end to end

milestone

One network — BNB Chain: cheap, fast, EVM — from invoice creation through settlement and webhook. This phase proves the product works.

  • ForwarderFactory executed on a real EVM, proving off-chain CREATE2 derivation matches on-chain deployment
  • Watcher with cursor persistence, reorg detection, and an RPC pool with fallback
  • Payout address management with a second factor and a 24-hour delay any member can cancel
  • Settlement runner with cost ceiling, spend cap and stuck-transaction replacement
  • Webhook delivery with exponential backoff and full jitter
  • Unmatched payments routed to an exception queue

exit criterion — partially met The full loop — derive, pay, detect, credit, settle, merchant paid — is proved on a real EVM in CI. What remains is a mainnet run, which is blocked on a ChainSigner backed by a KMS. Two reorg bugs were found and fixed here: the shallowest rather than deepest mismatch was being returned, and the block memory was never filled beyond the rewind depth.

05done

Checkout page

complete

The end customer's experience. This is where conversion is won or lost.

  • Self-contained single file with a hand-written QR encoder (byte mode, EC level M, versions 1–6)
  • Network choice ranked by FeePolicy, showing each network's settlement cost
  • Address, memo, copy-to-clipboard with a graceful fallback when the clipboard is denied
  • Rate lock as a depleting rule rather than a digital clock
  • Underpaid path, and a blocking warning on memo-based chains
  • AVEX's palette: black ground, lime accent, monospace numbers

exit criterion — partially met Rendered and verified in Chromium at 1280px and 390px. Still outstanding: a physical scan test on a real phone with a real wallet, and the mobile refinement. Not shown to a payer before that scan test passes.

Risks that must be visible in the code

RiskWhy it mattersContainmentState
Payout address change An attacker changes the address and takes every future payment. The single most important attack vector. Second factor, email confirmation, 24-hour delay, all members notified, and any member can cancel built
Counterfeit contract A worthless token named USDT gains real standing Curated list, automatic probe, manual review; clean checks never accumulate into approval built
Oracle error A wrong rate means goods handed over for less than they cost, silently Median of several sources, staleness gate, per-asset circuit breaker built
Reorg and double credit A confirmed transaction can be reversed Idempotency key chain:txHash:transferIndex under a database constraint; amountPaid recomputed from surviving rows, never incremented built
Gas wallet drain The only sensitive key; emptying it halts all settlement KMS, per-transaction ceiling, rolling spend cap, balance alerting as runway, hot/warm separation signer pending
Stablecoin freeze Tether and Circle can lock any address with a single call — no court and no jurisdiction involved An address per invoice, forwarded immediately, never a shared pool. This is the strongest argument against pooled custody. built
RPC geography Large providers block some regions Own node plus a fallback pool; rpcUrl is configurable per chain built
Telegram ban Platform rules do not care where a company is registered; enforcement is removal, not litigation Surface separation and a feature flag on which assets are displayed phase 10
Crypto refunds The sending address may be an exchange's hot wallet; refunding to it loses the money Mandatory destination confirmation and a warning on known exchange addresses phase 7
09

What I need from you

The three decisions this section originally asked for have been answered: no swap in v1, Bitcoin dropped, BNB Chain first with Tron built last. What is open now affects phase 6 and beyond.

  • The real domain. The subdomains are placeholders. The admin panel needs its own origin, so this shapes phase 6 directly.
  • The reseller commission model. A share of the AVEX fee, or a share of volume? The first carries less regulatory risk and is easier to make auditable.
  • How AX relates to AVEX Pay. Can a merchant accept AX? If so it becomes a fixed_rate asset on the curated list, on a different path from general crypto.

Two technical items are outstanding regardless: a ChainSigner backed by a KMS, which is what blocks a real mainnet settlement, and a physical QR scan test before the checkout is shown to a payer.
