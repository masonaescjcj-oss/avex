AAVEX Pay
API reference v1

Accept crypto in about twenty lines

Two ways in. Open a checkout and send the customer a link — we handle the currency choice, the address, the QR and the waiting. Or open an invoice yourself if you already know which coin they are paying in and want to render the payment page inside your own app.

0.5% of what you process, and nothing else. No monthly fee, no minimum, no setup cost, and no charge for a month you took no payments in. The commission comes out of the payment on chain as it is swept to you — 0.45% above $50,000 a month, 0.4% above $250,000 — so there is never an invoice from us to pay, and nothing about your account with us can stop your checkout working. You choose whether you absorb it or your customer does; see who pays the commission.

Everything below is the same API in test and live mode. Start with a test key, drive your own webhook handler end to end with simulate-payment, then swap the key. Nothing else changes.

🔑 Authentication

A bearer token on every request. Two kinds exist and they are not interchangeable: an API key for your server, and a session for a human in the dashboard. 

# Your server. The key carries its own mode and its own permissions.
curl https://api.avex.example/v1/organizations/ORG_ID/invoices \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer ak_live_…"

Keys are shown once, at creation, and stored only as a hash — we cannot recover one for you. Scope each key to what it needs: a key that opens invoices does not need settings:write, and an integration that only reads should not be able to move your payout address.

Never put a key in a browser, a mobile app or anything a customer can read. Nothing in this API needs one client-side — the payer endpoints below take no credentials at all, which is exactly why.

🧪 Test and live

A key's prefix is its mode: ak_test_… or ak_live_…. Every invoice and checkout carries the mode it was created in, and echoes it back so your logs can never be ambiguous about which one you are looking at.

ak_test_…Only ever creates test objects. Asking for mode: "live" in the body changes nothing.
ak_live_…Only ever creates live objects, for the same reason in reverse.
sessionMay pass mode explicitly. Defaults to live.

That rule is one-directional on purpose. A key you paste into staging, CI or a third-party integration must not be able to take real money even if something asks it to — and a live key must not be able to quietly stop charging your customers.

A test invoice gets a deposit address that is not valid on any chain, so nothing can be sent to it by accident. Pay it with simulate-payment instead. Test volume never appears in your reports and never counts towards your bill.

💱 Amounts, and why they are strings

Every amount in this API is an integer in a string. Fiat is micro-dollars — "20000000" is $20.00. Crypto is the asset's smallest unit — "20100502512562814071" is 20.1005… USDT at eighteen decimals.

Not a style choice. Eighteen decimals does not fit in a double, and a gateway that rounded a balance would misreport what you are owed. Parse these with a big-integer type, never with parseFloat.

Amounts owed always round up to the asset's precision, so you are never left a fraction short of the figure you asked for. Valuations round down.

🧮 Who pays the commission

Our cut is taken on chain, by the contract that sweeps the payment to you: it sends us the commission and you the rest, in one transaction. There is no second transfer available, so "the customer pays the fee" cannot mean one. It means the invoice asks for more, so that what is left after the split is the price you quoted.

Set a default with POST /v1/organizations/:orgId/commission/fee-payer, and override it for one order by sending feePayer when you create an invoice or a checkout. A checkout that sends nothing follows your default at the moment the customer picks a currency, not the moment you created the link — a link can sit unopened for an hour, and a change you made in between was meant to apply.

merchantdefaultThe customer sends your price. You receive it less the commission.
payeropt inThe customer sends your price plus the commission. You receive your price.
# $20.00 of USDT, at 0.5%, with the customer paying the fee
{
  "assetId": "…",
  "amountFiatMicros": "20000000",
  "feePayer": "payer"
}

# 201 Created — amountDue is grossed up, amountNet is what reaches you
{
  "amountDue": "20106564793405046747",
  "amountNet": "20000000000000000000",
  "feeBps": 53,
  "networkFeeBps": 3,
  "feePayer": "payer"
}

The transfer costs something, and that part is always the customer's. feeBps above is 53 rather than your 50, and the three is networkFeeBps: what the chain charged to move this particular payment, measured when the invoice was created and expressed as a share of it. A $20 order on BNB Chain at a quiet moment is issued for about $20.01; the same order on TRON is issued for $20.00, because there the payment lands in your own wallet and we send nothing.

It is not subject to feePayer, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. The commission is a price you may absorb as a courtesy to your customer; absorbing the cost of the transfer would mean paying to be paid. It is not our revenue either — it reimburses the wallet that pays for your settlements.

It is a rate rather than a fixed few cents because it has to be. The deposit address is a hash over the contract's own arguments and the rate is one of them, so what is charged is fixed at the moment the address exists and can never be revisited. A fixed fee would need a second transfer, and there is nobody to send it.

The customer is told. On the hosted checkout the commission appears as its own line, because a customer charged more than your price and not told why has been overcharged as far as they can tell. If your own payment page renders the invoice yourself, show it the same way — amountDue and amountNet give you the figures.

Two things this does not change. Our cut is the same either way, so this is your pricing decision and not ours. And it is snapshotted on the invoice: changing your default never moves the amount on an invoice already issued, because a payer may already be looking at it.

On an asset we take no commission on — Telegram Stars — feePayer: "payer" changes nothing at all. There is no fee to pass on, and we will not invent one for you to keep.

On TRON it works, but not on chain. There the payment goes straight into your own wallet and no contract of ours is in the path, so there is nothing to split. The commission is charged to your balance instead, and feePayer: "payer" still grosses the invoice up — you keep your price and the commission you owe cancels out.

Which also makes TRON the one chain with no networkFeeBps at all. Nothing of ours moves the money, so there is no transfer for anyone to pay for.

The minimum, and why it moves

Each chain has a smallest order it can carry, derived from live gas rather than set in a table: roughly two hundred and fifty times what one settlement costs there. At a quiet tenth of a gwei that is about $1.43 on BNB Chain and $11 to $17 on Ethereum, where one settlement costs four or five cents at today's gas; below it, POST /v1/invoices answers 422 amount_below_minimum and the hosted checkout shows that network as unavailable rather than letting a customer pick it.

The reason is not the gas itself — your customer covered that. It is that the charge is fixed when the invoice is created and the gas is paid when it settles, so a chain that got dearer in between leaves a gap. On a large order the commission absorbs it; on a small one it cannot, and we would rather refuse the order than take it and discover that later.

TRON and TON have no such floor, down to fifty cents. There is no settlement to pay for, so nothing about the order size can go wrong — which is what those chains are for, and where small orders belong.

👛 Your own wallets, on TRON

On most networks we hand each invoice its own address, derived so that funds sent there can only reach you. TRON works differently: a transfer there carries no note, so one address cannot say which invoice it was for, and giving every invoice its own contract would cost more in network fees than the payment is often worth.

So on TRON you register a handful of your own addresses — three to five is plenty — and we hand them out. The payer's transfer goes straight into your wallet: no contract of ours in the path, nothing to sweep, and no network fee for us to pass on. Each invoice asks for a very slightly different amount, a few hundredths of a cent apart, and that is how two payments to one address are told apart.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/deposit-wallets
walletsYour pool. A retired wallet stays listed — invoices already open on it are still paid.
pendingAdditions waiting out their 24 hours.
POST/v1/organizations/:orgId/deposit-wallets
chainrequired — a pooled network. Anything else is refused: set a payout address for those instead.
addressrequired — your own address. Checked, so a mistyped one is refused rather than accepted.
labelYour name for it, so a support conversation can refer to one.
DELETE/v1/organizations/:orgId/deposit-wallets/:walletId
—Retires it. Immediate, and invoices already open on it are still credited.
DELETE/v1/organizations/:orgId/deposit-wallets/pending/:changeId
—Cancels a scheduled addition. Any member may do this.

The first wallet is immediate; every one after it waits 24 hours. These are addresses your customers' money lands in, so adding one is protected exactly as changing a payout address is: owner-only, your second factor proven again, a day before it takes effect, and an email to everyone in your organization in between. Any of them can cancel it. The first wallet on a network is immediate because there is nothing to redirect — without one you cannot take payments on that network at all.

Retiring is immediate. Deliberately not symmetrical: adding an address can redirect money and waits; taking one away can only stop money arriving somewhere, and if you have spotted a wrong address you should not have to wait a day to stop using it.

Our commission on these payments is a balance. We are not in the path, so there is nothing for us to take a cut of as it passes — it is charged to your account instead and cleared by your next invoice on another network.

📉 Your balance

On most chains the commission never touches your books: the contract that sweeps a payment to you takes our cut in the same transaction, and there is nothing to settle afterwards. TRON is different, and the difference is in your favour — the payer sends to your own wallet, so there is no contract of ours in the path, no sweep, and no network fee for us to pass on.

The commission on those payments has to be collected some other way, so it is charged to a balance. Take $100 on TRON at 0.5% and your balance reads -500000 — fifty cents, in micro-dollars, owed to us.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/balance
balanceUsdMicrosSigned. Negative means you owe us.
creditLimitUsdMicrosHow far the balance may go before invoicing on accruing chains stops.
canInvoiceOnAccruingChainsfalse once the limit is passed.
entriesThe most recent 50, newest first — every movement, with the invoice it came from.
# GET /v1/organizations/:orgId/balance
{
  "balanceUsdMicros": "-500000",
  "creditLimitUsdMicros": "500000000",
  "canInvoiceOnAccruingChains": true,
  "entries": [
    { "kind": "accrual",  "amountUsdMicros": "-500000", "invoiceId": "…" }
  ]
}

How it is settled. Your next invoice on a chain where the cut can be taken on chain carries a slightly higher fee, and the extra clears the balance. It is capped at 2% on top of your own rate, so an invoice never has a surprising amount taken out of it — a large balance is cleared over several invoices rather than out of one. The raise applies to you and never to your customer: it is your balance, and grossing it onto a payer would charge them for it.

accrual−Commission on a payment we could not take a cut of.
recovery+Cleared by the raised fee on a later invoice.
settlement+You paid us directly.
adjustment±A correction, with a reason recorded.

A reversed payment reverses its commission. If a reorg takes a payment away, the accrual it created is undone. You are never left owing us a cut of a sale that did not happen.

There is a limit. Past $500 owed, new invoices on chains that accrue are refused with 402 balance_owed until the balance is settled. Chains where the commission is taken on chain keep working — those invoices are what clears it.

🧾 Checkouts — the shortest path

You know the amount; only your customer knows which coin they hold. A checkout holds the amount and lets them choose, which is why you do not have to pick an asset up front.

POST/v1/organizations/:orgId/checkouts
amountFiatMicrosrequired — micro-dollars. "25000000" is $25.00.
referenceYour order id. Also the idempotency key — see below.
descriptionShown to the customer above the amount.
successUrlWhere to send them once the payment is final.
cancelUrlWhere to send them if they give up.
ttlMsHow long they have to choose. One minute to one day; one hour by default.
modeSession credentials only. An API key's mode always wins.
curl -X POST https://api.avex.example/v1/organizations/ORG_ID/checkouts \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer ak_test_…" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"amountFiatMicros":"25000000","reference":"order-1042","description":"Order 1042"}'

# 201 Created
{
  "id": "9c1f…",
  "url": "https://pay.avex.example/pay/9c1f…",
  "status": "open",
  "mode": "test",
  "amountFiatMicros": "25000000",
  "expiresAt": "2026-08-18T10:04:00.000Z"
}

Send the customer to url. Retrying with the same reference returns the same checkout and a 200 instead of a 201 — so a dropped response never becomes two payment links for one order, only one of which will ever be marked paid.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/checkouts/:sessionId

The checkout as you see it, including invoiceId once the customer has chosen. Poll this, or take the webhook — the webhook is better.

POST/v1/organizations/:orgId/checkouts/:sessionId/cancel

Withdraw a checkout before it is paid. A paid checkout cannot be cancelled: the money has arrived, and pretending otherwise would leave your customer looking at a cancelled page having paid.

⭐ Telegram Stars

If you sell inside Telegram, Stars can sit alongside your crypto orders — one invoice model, one dashboard, one webhook stream. What we do for Stars is narrower than for crypto, and the difference is structural rather than something we will fill in later, so it is worth reading before you build.

Your bot takes the Stars, not us. Stars paid to a bot land in that bot's own Telegram balance. There is no address, no chain and nothing for a third party to intercept — so AVEX is the record, not the custodian. We cannot hold them, settle them, or take a percentage of them, and we do not pretend to: a Stars invoice always carries feeBps: 0.

Setting up

Enable the XTR currency and set your own rate for it. Nothing prices Stars — Telegram sells them in bundles at varying rates — so the rate is yours, the same way it is for a token you issued. Stars are whole units, so an invoice too small to express in them is refused rather than rounded up.

Taking a payment

POST/v1/organizations/:orgId/invoices

The same route as any invoice, with the XTR asset. You get back a depositAddress of the form telegram:<uuid> — not an address, a payload. Put it in invoice_payload when your bot calls Telegram's createInvoiceLink with currency: "XTR".

POST /v1/organizations/:orgId/invoices/:invoiceId/telegram-payment
chargeIdrequired — telegram_payment_charge_id from successful_payment. Also the idempotency key.
amountStarsrequired — total_amount from the same object.
payloadinvoice_payload. Checked against the invoice when sent, which catches a payment reported against the wrong order.
# In your bot's successful_payment handler
curl -X POST https://api.avex.example/v1/organizations/ORG_ID/invoices/INVOICE_ID/telegram-payment \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer ak_live_…" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"chargeId":"...","amountStars":"200","payload":"telegram:4d2a..."}'

Safe to call twice. Telegram does not promise an update arrives exactly once, so the charge id is the idempotency key — a repeat answers alreadyRecorded: true and credits nothing further. Reporting the same charge against a different invoice is refused with 422 charge_reused: that is an integration bug, not a retry, and treating it as one would mark a second order paid against the first one's money.

What this means for your figures

The evidence for a Stars payment is your API key and nothing else — there is no chain for us to read. So Stars are recorded as self-reported and contribute no priced volume to your reports. They are visible as what they are rather than blended into figures we can stand behind.

🌐 Payer endpoints no credentials

The hosted checkout page uses these, and you can too if you are building your own. They take no authentication — the session id in the URL is the whole authorisation, so treat that link as a secret and send it only to the customer it belongs to.

Nothing here reveals your payout address, your commission or your other orders. Browser access is allowed only from origins you register with us.

GET/pay/:sessionId/statepublic

The amount, your business name, the status, and — once a currency is chosen — the address to pay. Poll this to follow the payment.

GET/pay/:sessionId/optionspublic

Every currency and chain you can be paid in, with the exact amount for each at a rate we will honour. An asset we cannot price right now comes back available: false with a reason rather than disappearing.

POST/pay/:sessionId/selectpublic
assetIdrequired — from /options.

Creates the invoice and returns the address. Picking the same asset twice is a no-op. Switching before anything is sent gives a new address; switching after money is on its way is refused with 409 locked.

🧾 Receipts no credentials

Every checkout has a receipt at {url}/receipt. Creating a checkout returns it as receiptUrl, so you can file it with the order straight away — it answers 409 not_paid until the payment lands. Once it does, the payer reaches it from the payment page themselves, with no sign-in.

GET/pay/:sessionId/receipt

What was bought, from whom, for how much, in which asset on which chain, and the transaction hashes. The hashes are the point: everything else on a receipt is something we assert, and those are something the payer can check against a public chain without trusting either of us.

An overpayment gets a receipt, and it says so. The money arrived, so the payer is entitled to the record — but it names the excess and says you owe them the difference, rather than printing as a clean settlement. An underpayment gets no receipt at all: the bill is not settled, and a document that looked like one would be worse than none.

A test receipt says it is a test, above everything else on the page. Nothing was sent — a test invoice's address is valid on no chain — and a document headed "Paid in full" is exactly the thing somebody files and later produces as proof of a payment that never happened.

Take the webhook rather than polling. Every delivery is signed; the secret is shown once when you create the endpoint.

Verifying a delivery

Compare in constant time, and check the timestamp. A signature check that leaks timing, or that accepts an hour-old payload, is not a signature check. 

# Node
const expected = crypto
  .createHmac('sha256', secret)
  .update(`${timestamp}.${rawBody}`)
  .digest('hex');

if (!crypto.timingSafeEqual(Buffer.from(expected), Buffer.from(signature))) reject();
if (Math.abs(Date.now() / 1000 - timestamp) > 300) reject();

Deliveries retry, so your handler must be idempotent — key on the invoice id and the status, not on having received the request. And read the amount, not just the status: an underpaid invoice has real money against it.

⚙️ Before your first live payment

Three things, or invoices will be refused with a reason saying which.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/assets

Everything you could accept, with whether you have enabled it, its pricing mode and its review verdict. Only approved assets can be invoiced in.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/payout-addresses

Where your money goes, per chain. You need one on every chain you accept — funds can only ever move to an address you configured.

POST/v1/organizations/:orgId/payout-addresses

A change here is scheduled rather than immediate, and we email you. That delay is the difference between a stolen session costing you one order and costing you everything after it.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/commission

Your rate, the next rung on the ladder, and what you have processed this period. There is no monthly fee and no minimum: 0.5% of what you process, 0.45% above $50,000 a month, 0.4% above $250,000. The rate is reviewed when a month closes and moves in both directions.

POST/v1/organizations/:orgId/commission/fee-payer

{ "feePayer": "merchant" | "payer" }. Your default for new invoices; see who pays the commission. Invoices already issued keep the amounts they were created with.

GET/v1/organizations/:orgId/reports/volume

Live volume by chain and asset, counted from credited unreversed payments rather than from invoices marked paid. The two differ after a reorg, and this is the one you should reconcile against.

GET/v1/prices

The rates we are willing to quote, and which sources back them. A rate we cannot corroborate across sources is not returned at all.

GET/healthpublic

For your monitoring. No credentials.

🚦 Errors

Every failure is { "error": "code", "message": "…" }. Branch on error; show message to a human. The status tells you which of you has to act.

401unauthenticatedNo credential, or not a valid one.
403forbiddenThe credential is real but lacks the permission.
404not_foundAlso what you get for something belonging to another merchant — confirming it exists would leak the id space.
409asset_disabledEnable it first.
409asset_unapprovedStill in review, or blocked.
409asset_unlistedWe have stopped accepting that currency — our side, not yours. Invoices already open still complete.
409no_payout_addressAdd one for that chain.
409fixed_rate_expiredYour own rate lapsed. Set a current one.
409not_test_modeThat only works on a test invoice.
409not_paidYou asked for a receipt before the payment landed. The link is real — come back once it has.
409not_starsThat invoice is not in Stars. A crypto invoice is paid on chain.

422payload_mismatchThe Telegram payload belongs to a different invoice.
409lockedA payment is already on its way; the currency cannot change.

422amount_invalidZero, negative, both amounts at once, or an asset too coarse to express it.
422amount_below_minimumToo small for that chain right now. The message names the figure; a cheaper chain has no such floor.

AVEX Pay is non-custodial. Every deposit address is derived so that it can only pay the payout address you configured, and that binding is in the address itself rather than in a promise — we could not redirect your funds if we wanted to.
