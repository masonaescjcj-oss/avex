The address is the guarantee

Most gateways ask you to trust that they will forward your money. There is nothing to trust here, because there is no moment at which we have it.

A deposit address is computed from three things: the invoice, your payout wallet, and our fee. Nothing is deployed there — the contract that will live at that address does not exist yet, and when it is created it can do exactly one thing, which is pay the wallet that went into the hash. Change the wallet and you get a different address. We cannot point yours somewhere else without producing an address your customer never funded.

  1. 01

    You open an invoice

    We compute the address and hand it back. No contract exists at it. No key controls it.

  2. 02

    Your customer sends the money

    Straight to that address, from any wallet or exchange. We watch the chain for it.

  3. 03

    We tell you, and settle

    A signed webhook fires. Then one transaction creates the contract and empties it.

  4. 04

    The split happens on chain

    Your wallet gets the payment less our fee, and we get the fee. Same transaction, no intermediate balance.

What this does not protect you from. Stablecoins are freezable by the company that issues them — Tether and Circle can both blacklist an address, and no gateway changes that. What non-custodial removes is us: we cannot hold your money, delay it, or send it elsewhere.

Quickstart

start here

One request, one link. Amounts are strings of micro-dollars — never floats, because 19.99 has no exact binary representation and a shop at that price would be a cent out. 

# Ask for $19.99, and get a link to send your customer
curl -X POST https://api.avex.example/v1/organizations/$ORG/checkouts \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $AVEX_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"amountFiatMicros":"19990000","reference":"order-1042"}'

# 201 Created
{
  "id": "c0ffee00-…",
  "url": "https://pay.avex.example/pay/c0ffee00-…",
  "receiptUrl": "https://pay.avex.example/pay/c0ffee00-…/receipt",
  "mode": "live"
}

Send them url. File receiptUrl with the order — it answers 409 not_paid until the money lands, then becomes the receipt your customer keeps.

Test mode

no faucet, no testnet

Start with a key whose prefix is ak_test_. Everything behaves the same, except the deposit addresses are not valid on any chain — nothing can be sent to one by accident. Pay them from the API and your webhook handler runs exactly as it would for real money. 

# Drive your own handler end to end without spending anything
curl -X POST https://api.avex.example/v1/organizations/$ORG/invoices/$ID/simulate-payment \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $AVEX_TEST_KEY"

A test key cannot open a live invoice however it asks, and a live key cannot opt into test mode. Every invoice and every webhook says which it is, so a key shipped to production by mistake is visible in your own logs.

Webhooks

verify before you act

Only a signed webhook should complete an order. The page your customer returns to is a URL anybody can visit, so it must never change an order's status. 

# avex-signature: t=1787048882,v1=9f2c41ab…
# Sign "<t>.<raw body>" with your endpoint secret, compare in constant time,
# and reject a timestamp more than 300 seconds old.

signed = f"{t}.{raw_body}"
expected = hmac.new(secret, signed.encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
if not hmac.compare_digest(expected, v1): return 400

Compare the whole string in constant time — == on a signature leaks how much of a guess was right. Retries are signed afresh, so a replayed old delivery fails the timestamp check rather than the signature one.

What to expect back

Every failure is { "error": "code", "message": "…" }. Branch on error; show message to a person. The status says which of us has to act.

CodeWhat it meansWhose move
no_payout_address Add a wallet for that chain. Without one there is nowhere for the money to go. yours
asset_unlisted We have stopped accepting that currency. Invoices already open still complete. ours
price_unavailable No rate we trust right now. Retryable — we would rather refuse than misprice you. ours
fixed_rate_expired The rate you set for your own token lapsed. Set a current one. yours

There is no 402: nothing about your account with us can refuse you an invoice. Your terms are on the dashboard, current, rather than in a page somebody would have to remember to update.
